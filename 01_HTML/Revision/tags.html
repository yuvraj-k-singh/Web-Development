How to Become a Frontend Developer

Learn more about the typical requirements associated with a career as a front-end web developer. Additionally, explore the usual tasks you will perform, the education and skills you need, and the earning potential for this position.


Web development careers can take one of three paths. This article will focus on how you can become a front-end developer responsible for building a website's user-facing and interactive components. Alternatively, back-end developers specialize in the behind-the-scenes features that help a website run. In contrast, full-stack developers perform the duties of both front-end and back-end web developers.

Front-end developers possess various skills, including coding and working with multiple languages like JavaScript and SQL. You have several options for how you go about acquiring them. Let’s explore this career and the paths you may take if you’re considering becoming a front-end developer.


What does a front-end web developer do?

The front-facing component of a website that the user interacts with is the work of a front-end web developer. Front-end developers build anything you view on your computer or device screen. Ultimately, a quality final product should look great, have aesthetics that match the project’s objectives, and provide user-friendly functionality. Here’s a look at some of the everyday responsibilities and tasks you will perform as a front-end developer:

  • Debugging to maintain a website’s functionality
  • Designing components for mobile sites and various browsers
  • Ensuring websites can operate at high speeds
  • Building website prototypes

How to become a front-end web developer: 4 steps

Setting yourself up for a successful career as a front-end web developer requires a combination of the right skills, education, and the ability to demonstrate your experience. Follow these steps to learn how:

Step 1: Get an education

Although front-end web developers typically hold a bachelor’s degree, you can enter the field without one. Earning a bachelor’s degree in an area of study such as computer science is a great way to gain coding skills, learn theoretical concepts, and develop a portfolio. However, spending several years in school isn’t the best option for everyone. You can accelerate the process of becoming a front-end web developer by enrolling in a boot camp, either full-time or part-time. This will allow you to develop in-demand skills over six months or less. Another way to acquire front-end web development skills is through online classes, where you can learn at your own pace.


Step 2: Develop skills

Qualifying for a front-end web developer position primarily comes down to acquiring the right skills to do the job. This position requires a skill set filled with technical abilities and various workplace skills.

Technical Skills

  • HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for creating dynamic web pages. These are three of the programming languages considered vital for web development.
  • SQL and NoSQL for interacting with data within relational and non-relational databases.
  • GIT for version control allows you to work with other team members and document projects.
  • Graphic design tools like Photoshop and Figma, and prototyping tools like InVision and Pidoco.
  • Knowledge of various libraries and design frameworks, including React and jQuery.

Step 3: Build a portfolio

Building a portfolio is a great way to demonstrate to employers that you have the necessary skills and experience working on relevant projects. In your portfolio, you can show projects highlighting your front-end web developer skills, websites you built, and other relevant information. Any examples that help illustrate your experience with programming, design, and various technical skills would be helpful when you put together your portfolio.


Step 4: Earn certificates

Another way to demonstrate your technical prowess to employers and stand out amongst other candidates is by earning certificates. Several universities offer opportunities to earn certificates like Harvard's Front End Web Development Graduate Certificate or the University of Washington's Front-End Development with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript certificate. You can also earn front-end web development Professional Certificates on Coursera, such as the Meta Front-End Developer Professional Certificate.


Front-end web developer salary and job outlook

According to data from the US Bureau of Labour Statistics, the average front-end web developer salary is $92,750 [1]. This field also boasts a strong outlook, with web development positions projected to grow 8 percent from 2023 to 2033 [1]. Some top locations for web developers looking to earn a high income include Washington, Oregon, California, and Utah [2]. Other ways to increase your earning potential include completing certifications, such as the Certified Web Professional (CWP) credential offered by the International Web Association or a Professional Software Developer Certification from the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) Computer Society. Additionally, you might consider furthering your education with an advanced degree and gaining more experience over time.


Conclusion

Front-end web development is a rewarding career path for those interested in building the user-facing components of websites. By acquiring the necessary skills, education, and experience, you can position yourself for success in this field. Whether through formal education, boot camps, or self-directed learning, there are multiple pathways to becoming a proficient front-end developer.
